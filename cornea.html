What is the Cornea?

The cornea is the clear, transparent front portion of the eye that allows light to enter and, along with the lens, helps focus light onto the the retina to form an an image. Keratoconus is derived from the Greek words kerato, meaning cornea, and conus, meaning cone-shaped. It is characterised by progressive thinning of the cornea, causing it to change from its normal dome shape to a cone-like shape, which can result in blurred or distorted vision. Environmental and genetic factors may contribute to the condition, which is often associated with excessive eye rubbing and certain conditions such as retinitis pigmentosa and Ehlers-Danlos syndrome. Keratoconus commonly develops during the teenage years.

Efficient Management of a Broad Range of Corneal Diseases

Our cornea specialists provide evaluation and treatment for various corneal conditions affecting vision and eye comfort.

Corneal Ulcer

A corneal ulcer is an infection of the cornea that can cause inflammation, redness, pain and ulcer formation. It may occur due to infections following an eye injury, contact lens-related infections or certain nutritional deficiencies.

Eye Trauma

An injury to the eye can damage the cornea and may result in a corneal tear or other structural damage. Corneal injuries may require urgent evaluation and, in severe cases, surgical repair.

Viral Keratitis

Viral keratitis is a corneal infection caused by viruses such as herpes simplex and varicella-zoster virus. It can cause redness, pain, watering and inflammation and may recur in some patients if not appropriately managed.

Corneal Dystrophies

Corneal dystrophies are a group of conditions that can have a genetic component and cause abnormal material to accumulate within the cornea. This may make the normally clear cornea cloudy and affect vision. Examples include epithelial basement membrane dystrophy, Fuchs' corneal dystrophy and macular corneal dystrophy.

Dry Eye

Dry eye is a common condition associated with inadequate tear production or excessive tear evaporation. Prolonged screen use, environmental factors and certain systemic or hormonal conditions may contribute to symptoms such as dryness, burning, redness and a foreign-body sensation.

Congenital Corneal Diseases

Some corneal conditions may be present at birth due to genetic disorders, developmental abnormalities or infections during pregnancy. Conditions may include certain corneal dystrophies, congenital glaucoma and Peters anomaly.

Advanced Technology for Accurate Corneal Diagnosis

At Khanna Eye Centre, advanced diagnostic equipment helps our specialists evaluate the structure, shape and health of the cornea.

Corneal Topography

Evaluates the shape and curvature of the corneal surface.

Pentacam

Provides detailed three-dimensional analysis of the cornea and anterior segment.

Anterior Segment OCT

Provides detailed imaging of the anterior eye and can help assess corneal layers, opacities and epithelial thickness.

Corneal Transplant Surgery (Keratoplasty)

Corneal transplant surgery involves removing and replacing damaged or diseased corneal tissue with healthy donor tissue. Depending on the condition and the layers of the cornea affected, our cornea surgeon may recommend a full-thickness or partial-thickness transplant.

Penetrating Keratoplasty (PK)

Penetrating keratoplasty involves replacing the full thickness of a diseased or damaged cornea with healthy donor corneal tissue.

DALK (Deep Anterior Lamellar Keratoplasty)

DALK is a partial-thickness corneal transplant procedure in which the diseased anterior layers of the cornea are replaced with healthy donor tissue while preserving the patient's healthy deeper corneal layers.

Endothelial Keratoplasty (DSEK/DMEK)

Endothelial keratoplasty is a partial-thickness corneal transplant technique that replaces the diseased inner endothelial layer of the cornea with healthy donor tissue. DSEK and DMEK are commonly used for selected conditions affecting the corneal endothelium.

Contact lens related issues

Contact lenses can affect the health and comfort of the cornea if they are not fitted, cleaned or used appropriately.

Corneal abrasion Discomfort, watering and sensitivity to light

Other Corneal Surgeries

Our cornea services also include surgical procedures for selected corneal surface, traumatic and ocular surface conditions.

Pterygium Surgery with Sutureless Autograft

Pterygium surgery involves removing the wing-shaped growth extending onto the cornea and covering the affected area with a conjunctival autograft using a sutureless technique.

Corneal Tear Repair

Corneal tears caused by trauma require prompt assessment and, when necessary, surgical repair to restore the integrity of the cornea.

Amniotic Membrane Grafting

Amniotic membrane grafting may be used in selected cases of ocular surface damage, including certain chemical injuries such as exposure to lime or chuna.

Stem Cell Transplant (SLET)

Simple Limbal Epithelial Transplantation (SLET) is a procedure used in selected cases of limbal stem cell deficiency to help restore the corneal surface.

Surface Tumour Excision and Biopsy

Abnormal growths on the ocular surface may be surgically removed and, when required, sent for biopsy to determine their nature.

Dry Eye – Punctal Plugs

Punctal plugs may be used in selected patients with dry eye to reduce tear drainage and help retain natural tears on the eye surface for longer.
