Overview

Where Neurology Meets Ophthalmology

Neuro-ophthalmology is a subspecialty that merges the fields of neurology and ophthalmology.

This branch deals with vision-related problems that occur as a result of a disease or disorder of the nervous system. Not every vision problem begins in the eye. Sometimes the cause lies in the optic nerve, the brain, or the pathways that connect them. A neuro-ophthalmologist is trained to look beyond the eye, identify these hidden causes, and guide you toward the right treatment.

Symptoms

Common Symptoms

Recognising these signs early can make a real difference to your vision and overall health.

✓
Sudden Loss of Vision

A rapid drop in vision in one or both eyes, whether partial or complete, is a medical emergency. It may point to a problem with the optic nerve, blood supply, or brain, and needs urgent evaluation.

✓
Transient Loss of Vision

Vision that blacks out or blurs for a few seconds or minutes and then returns should never be ignored. It can be an early warning of circulation problems or nerve-related disease.

✓
Diplopia (Double Vision)

Seeing two images of a single object can result from weakness or dysfunction of the nerves and muscles that control eye movement. It may affect balance, reading, and daily activities.

✓
Frequent Headaches with Vomiting

Persistent or worsening headaches, especially when accompanied by vomiting or visual disturbances, may indicate raised pressure around the brain and optic nerve. They need proper assessment.

✓
Loss of Colour Vision or Contrast Sensitivity

Colours that appear dull or faded, or difficulty distinguishing objects from their background, are often early signs of optic nerve disease, even when sharp vision seems normal.

✓
Visual Field Defects

Missing areas in your side or central vision, or frequently bumping into objects, can signal damage along the visual pathway. Specialised field testing helps locate the cause.

Conditions

Conditions We Diagnose and Manage

Our neuro-ophthalmology team evaluates and treats a wide range of nerve-related vision disorders.

Papilledema

Swelling of the optic disc caused by raised pressure inside the skull. It may cause headaches, brief vision blackouts, and nausea. Early detection is essential to prevent permanent vision loss.

Optic Neuritis

Inflammation of the optic nerve that often causes sudden blurred vision, pain on eye movement, and dull colours. It commonly affects one eye and may be linked to other neurological conditions.

Multiple Sclerosis

A condition in which the immune system affects the nerves of the brain and spinal cord. Vision problems such as optic neuritis or double vision are often among its first signs.

Optic Neuropathy

Damage to the optic nerve from reduced blood flow, compression, toxins, or other causes. It can lead to gradual or sudden vision loss and requires timely evaluation to protect the remaining vision.

How It Works

Our Process

A structured, step-by-step approach that takes you from symptoms to a clear diagnosis and treatment plan.

Detailed History and Consultation

We begin by listening carefully to your symptoms, their duration, and your medical background. Details about headaches, medications, and past illnesses help us understand what may be affecting your vision.

Comprehensive Eye Examination

Your vision, pupil responses, eye movements, colour vision, and eye pressure are checked thoroughly. We also examine the optic nerve and retina to look for signs of swelling or damage.

Specialised Diagnostic Tests

Tests such as visual field testing, OCT imaging, and contrast or colour vision assessment help us measure nerve function precisely. These tests give a clear picture of how your visual pathway is working.

Imaging and Coordination with Other Specialists

When needed, MRI or CT scans and blood tests may be advised. We work closely with neurologists, physicians, and other specialists to ensure a complete and well-coordinated evaluation.

Personalised Treatment Plan

Once the cause is identified, we explain your diagnosis and treatment options in simple language. Treatment may include medication, therapy, or surgical care, planned around your condition and lifestyle.

Monitoring and Long-Term Follow-Up

Many neuro-ophthalmic conditions need continued monitoring. Regular follow-up visits help us track recovery, adjust treatment, and protect your vision over the long term.

When to Visit

When to See a Neuro-Ophthalmologist

Certain vision changes should not wait. Early assessment can protect your sight and reveal underlying health concerns.

✓
Sudden or Unexplained Vision Changes

If you experience sudden blurring, loss of vision, or a dark patch in your sight, seek care immediately. Quick evaluation gives the best chance of preserving your vision.

✓
Double Vision or Eye Movement Problems

New double vision, drooping eyelids, or an eye that seems to drift or turn may indicate a nerve or muscle issue. These symptoms should be examined by a specialist without delay.

✓
Headaches with Visual Symptoms

Persistent headaches that come with nausea, vomiting, or blurred or fading vision may be a sign of increased pressure on the optic nerve. A neuro-ophthalmic evaluation can help find the cause.

✓
A Known Neurological Condition or Unexplained Symptoms

If you have multiple sclerosis, a history of stroke, or other neurological conditions, regular eye assessments are important. Likewise, if your eye doctor cannot explain your symptoms, a second opinion from a neuro-ophthalmologist can help.

Questions Answered

FAQs

When should I visit a neuro-ophthalmologist?
You should see a neuro-ophthalmologist if you have sudden or unexplained vision loss, double vision, a dark patch in your sight, or headaches along with blurred vision or vomiting. A referral is also helpful if you have a known neurological condition, or if your eye doctor cannot explain your symptoms. Sudden vision changes need urgent attention, so do not wait for them to settle on their own.
Is a neuro-visual disorder permanent?
It depends on the cause, how severe it is, and how early treatment begins. Some conditions, such as optic neuritis, may improve significantly with timely care, while others, such as certain forms of optic neuropathy, can cause lasting damage if treatment is delayed. Early diagnosis gives the best chance of protecting your vision, and your doctor can explain what to expect in your specific case.
How is a neuro-ophthalmology evaluation different from a regular eye check-up?
A regular eye check-up mainly looks at the health of the eye and your glasses power. A neuro-ophthalmology evaluation goes further and examines how the optic nerve, eye movements, pupil responses, and visual pathways are working. It may include visual field testing, OCT imaging, and colour vision assessment, and sometimes MRI or CT scans in coordination with other specialists.
Can headaches and vision problems be related?
Yes. Persistent headaches with blurred vision, brief vision blackouts, nausea, or vomiting can sometimes point to raised pressure around the brain and optic nerve, as seen in papilledema. Not every headache is serious, but when headaches come with visual symptoms, a proper evaluation is important to find the cause.
Will I need long-term follow-up after treatment?
Many neuro-ophthalmic conditions, such as optic neuritis and multiple sclerosis-related vision problems, need ongoing monitoring because symptoms can change or return. Regular follow-up visits help track recovery, adjust treatment, and catch changes early. Taking medicines as prescribed and keeping your general health in check also supports long-term vision protection.
