Conditions

Eyelid and Lacrimal Conditions We Treat

Khanna Eye Centre has years of experience in managing eyelid deformities and tear drainage problems. Our oculoplasty team evaluates each condition carefully and recommends suitable treatment based on its severity, symptoms, and impact on eye function.

Ectropion

Ectropion occurs when the eyelid turns outward, preventing it from sitting properly against the surface of the eye. It can lead to excessive watering, irritation, dryness, and difficulty keeping the eye adequately protected. Treatment is planned according to the underlying cause and severity of the eyelid changes.

Entropion

Entropion is a condition in which the eyelid turns inward, causing the eyelashes to rub against the surface of the eye. This may result in irritation, watering, redness, and discomfort. Appropriate treatment can help restore the normal position of the eyelid and protect the eye from continued irritation.

Ptosis

Ptosis refers to drooping of the upper eyelid, which may occur in one or both eyes. Depending on its severity, it can affect the appearance of the eyes and may interfere with the field of vision. Ptosis surgery may be considered to improve the position of the eyelid when clinically indicated.

Lacrimal Blockage

A blocked tear drainage system can cause persistent watering, discharge, and recurrent discomfort around the eye. Khanna Eye Centre provides lacrimal blockage procedures, including DCR (dacryocystorhinostomy) and DCT (dacryocystectomy), depending on the nature and location of the blockage.

Is It Right for You

Is Oculoplasty Right for You?

If a concern around your eyes affects how you see, feel, or look, an oculoplasty consultation can help you find the right solution.

✓
Your eyelids droop or feel heavy

If your upper eyelids sag enough to block part of your vision, you may find yourself tilting your head back or straining to keep your eyes open. This often leads to tired eyes and headaches by evening. Corrective surgery can lift the lid, restore a clear field of vision, and bring back a more open, natural look.

✓
Your eyes water constantly

Persistent watering, sticky discharge, or repeated eye infections are often signs of a blocked tear duct. Besides being uncomfortable and inconvenient, it can blur your vision and irritate the skin around the eyes. Modern procedures can reopen or bypass the blockage and restore normal tear drainage.

✓
Your eyelids turn in or out

When the eyelid rolls inward, the lashes rub against the eye, and when it turns outward, the eye is left exposed and dry. Both conditions cause redness, irritation, and watering, and can damage the cornea if ignored. A timely repair protects your vision and relieves ongoing discomfort.

✓
You have a lump or growth on the eyelid

Any new, growing, or changing lump, cyst, or patch on the eyelid should be examined by a specialist. While many are harmless, some need to be removed and tested to rule out serious conditions. Careful removal and reconstruction ensure the eyelid continues to function and look natural.

✓
Your eyes look bulging, sunken, or uneven

Changes in the position or appearance of your eyes can be linked to thyroid eye disease, past injury, or other orbital conditions. These changes may also cause dryness, double vision, or difficulty closing the eyes fully. An early evaluation helps identify the cause and plan the right treatment.

✓
You want a refreshed, natural look

Puffy lids, under-eye bags, excess skin, and fine lines can make you look tired even when you feel well rested. Aesthetic oculoplasty procedures address these concerns with precision and a focus on subtle, natural-looking results. The aim is to refresh your appearance, not change it.

Questions Answered

FAQs

What conditions does an oculoplasty specialist treat?
An oculoplasty specialist treats conditions of the eyelids, tear drainage system, and the structures around the eyes. At Khanna Eye Centre, this includes ectropion, entropion, ptosis, and blocked tear ducts, along with selected cosmetic concerns such as Botox and blepharoplasty. Prosthetic eye fitting is also available for patients who need it.
Is blepharoplasty only a cosmetic procedure?
Not always. Blepharoplasty can be done for cosmetic reasons, such as puffy or sagging eyelids, but it can also be recommended for functional reasons. When excess eyelid skin hangs low enough to block your field of vision, removing it can improve both sight and appearance. Your surgeon will assess which applies to you during consultation.
What is the difference between DCR and DCT for blocked tear ducts?
Both are procedures for lacrimal blockage, but they work differently. DCR (dacryocystorhinostomy) creates a new drainage pathway for tears to flow from the tear sac into the nose. DCT (dacryocystectomy) involves removing the tear sac and is considered in specific cases. The right option depends on the nature and location of the blockage, which your doctor will determine after examination.
Will I need surgery for every eyelid or tear duct problem?
No. Treatment depends on the cause, severity, and how much the problem affects your eye health and daily life. Some concerns, such as selected cosmetic issues, can be managed with non-surgical options like Botox, while others, such as significant ptosis or a blocked tear duct, may need surgery. A detailed evaluation helps decide the most suitable approach.
What should I expect during recovery after an oculoplasty procedure?
Recovery varies with the procedure, but mild swelling, bruising, or watering is common in the first few days. Our team gives clear instructions on medicines, eye care, and activities to avoid while you heal. Follow-up visits are scheduled to monitor your progress and address any concerns early. Always contact your doctor if you notice increasing pain, redness, or changes in vision.
