Understanding Glaucoma

Glaucoma, commonly known as “Kala Motia” in Hindi, is a group of eye conditions that can damage the optic nerve, often due to increased intraocular pressure. If left untreated, progressive optic nerve damage can result in permanent vision loss.

Who Is at Risk of Glaucoma?

Certain factors can increase the likelihood of developing glaucoma, making regular eye examinations particularly important for people at higher risk.

Age Above 40

The risk of glaucoma generally increases with age, making regular eye examinations important after the age of 40.

Family History

People with a family history of glaucoma may have a higher risk of developing the condition and should undergo regular eye examinations.

Myopia

People with myopia, or short-sightedness, may have an increased risk of developing certain types of glaucoma.

Increased Eye Pressure

Raised intraocular pressure can increase the risk of optic nerve damage, although glaucoma can also occur in people with normal eye pressure.

Previous Eye Injury

Trauma to the eye can damage the structures responsible for fluid drainage and may contribute to the development of glaucoma.

Long-Term Steroid Use

Prolonged use of corticosteroid medicines can increase intraocular pressure in some individuals and may contribute to steroid-induced glaucoma.

Types of Glaucoma

Glaucoma can occur in different forms depending on its underlying cause and the way fluid drains from the eye.

Open-Angle Glaucoma

Open-angle glaucoma often develops gradually without noticeable symptoms in its early stages. Without timely diagnosis and treatment, it can progressively damage the optic nerve and cause loss of peripheral vision.

Angle-Closure Glaucoma

Angle-closure glaucoma occurs when the drainage angle becomes narrowed or closed, causing a rapid rise in intraocular pressure. It may cause severe eye pain, redness, headache, blurred vision, halos around lights, or vomiting and requires immediate medical attention.

Congenital Glaucoma

Congenital glaucoma is a rare form of glaucoma that is present at birth and is associated with abnormal development of the eye's drainage system.

Other Forms of Glaucoma

Glaucoma may also develop as a result of certain medications, injuries or other underlying conditions.

Steroid-Induced Glaucoma

Long-term or inappropriate use of steroid medications can cause an increase in intraocular pressure in susceptible individuals.

Traumatic Glaucoma

An injury to the eye can damage the drainage structures and lead to increased eye pressure, potentially resulting in traumatic glaucoma.

Secondary Glaucoma

Glaucoma can sometimes develop as a complication of another eye condition, inflammation, medication use, or other underlying causes.

Paediatric Glaucoma

Glaucoma occurring in children requires specialised evaluation and management to protect the developing visual system.

Glaucoma Diagnosis

A comprehensive eye examination helps detect glaucoma, assess optic nerve damage and evaluate the risk of further vision loss.

Diagnostic Test Purpose
Visual Field Analysis (VFA) Evaluates the patient's field of vision and helps identify peripheral vision loss.
OCT RNFL Provides detailed imaging of the retinal nerve fibre layer to assess structural changes associated with glaucoma.
Pachymetry Measures the thickness of the cornea, which can help in interpreting intraocular pressure measurements.
Gonioscopy Examines the drainage angle of the eye and helps determine the type of glaucoma.

Glaucoma Treatment Options

Treatment is selected according to the type and severity of glaucoma, eye pressure, and the extent of optic nerve damage.

Medical Management

Prescription eye drops or other medicines may be used to lower intraocular pressure and help prevent further optic nerve damage.

Laser Treatment

Laser procedures may be recommended for selected types of glaucoma to improve fluid drainage or reduce intraocular pressure.

Trabeculectomy

Trabeculectomy is a surgical procedure that creates a new drainage pathway to help lower intraocular pressure when other treatment approaches are insufficient.

Glaucoma Valves and Implants

Glaucoma drainage devices or implants may be considered in certain patients when additional control of intraocular pressure is required.

Regular Monitoring

Glaucoma requires ongoing follow-up to monitor eye pressure, optic nerve health and visual fields and to assess the response to treatment.

Personalised Treatment Planning

The treatment approach is determined based on the patient's glaucoma type, stage, eye pressure, optic nerve condition, and overall eye health.
