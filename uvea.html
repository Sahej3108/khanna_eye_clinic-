Overview

Understanding Uveitis and Uvea Care

Uveitis is the inflammation of the middle layer of the eyeball, known as the uvea. The uvea is a separate speciality in ophthalmology, requiring in-depth knowledge of the uveal tract and good experience to treat these cases well. Uveitis generally requires long-term treatment with regular follow-ups, as the disease has a tendency to cause recurrences. With timely diagnosis and consistent care, inflammation can be controlled and vision protected.

Procedure

How is the Procedure Performed

Uveitis care follows a careful, step-by-step approach, from finding the cause to preventing recurrence.

Detailed History and Consultation

We begin by understanding your symptoms, their duration, past episodes, and medical history. Details about previous infections, joint problems, or systemic illnesses help us look for underlying causes.

Comprehensive Eye Examination

Your vision, eye pressure, and the front and back of the eye are examined in detail using a slit lamp and other instruments. This shows the location and severity of the inflammation.

Diagnostic Tests and Imaging

Tests such as OCT, FFA, and B-scan ultrasonography are used to assess the retina, macula, and deeper structures. These help us measure the extent of inflammation and detect complications.

Identifying the Underlying Cause

Blood tests, chest X-rays, or other investigations may be advised to look for infections or autoimmune conditions. We coordinate with physicians and rheumatologists when needed.

Targeted Treatment

Treatment usually includes anti-inflammatory eye drops, dilating drops, oral medicines, or injections, depending on the type and severity of uveitis. Complications like cataract, glaucoma, or macular swelling are managed as required.

Regular Follow-Up and Monitoring

Because uveitis can return, regular follow-up visits are essential. We adjust medicines gradually, monitor for recurrence, and protect your vision over the long term.

Symptoms

Symptoms

Uveitis commonly causes pain, redness, watering, and blurring of vision. The type depends on which part of the uvea is affected.

Anterior Uveitis

Inflammation at the front of the eye, involving the iris. It is the most common type and typically causes pain, redness, light sensitivity, and blurred vision.

Intermediate Uveitis

Inflammation in the middle part of the eye, mainly affecting the vitreous gel. It often causes floaters and blurred vision, usually with little pain or redness.

Posterior Uveitis

Inflammation at the back of the eye, involving the retina and choroid. It can cause blurred vision, floaters, and vision loss if not treated promptly.

Panuveitis

Inflammation involving all layers of the uvea, from front to back. It tends to be more severe and needs prompt, intensive treatment with close follow-up.

Diagnostics

Diagnostic Equipment for Retina Evaluation

Khanna Eye Centre in Delhi has the latest equipment for the diagnosis of various retina conditions.

✓
OCT (SD-OCT)

Gives detailed cross-sectional images of the retina and macula. It detects swelling and tracks treatment response.

✓
FFA (Fundus Fluorescein Angiography)

Uses a dye to highlight blood vessels in the retina. It shows leakage, blockage, and active inflammation.

✓
B-Scan Ultrasonography

Creates images of the back of the eye when the view is blocked, such as in cataract or vitreous haze.

✓
Indirect Ophthalmoscopy

Provides a wide view of the retina and periphery. It helps detect inflammation, tears, and detachments.

✓
Amsler Grid

A simple test to detect distortion or blind spots in central vision. It helps monitor macular problems.

✓
90D Biomicroscopy

Uses a special lens with a slit lamp for a detailed, magnified view of the retina and macula.

Questions Answered

FAQs

What is uveitis, and how serious is it?
Uveitis is inflammation of the uvea, the middle layer of the eyeball. It can range from mild to severe, and if left untreated, it may lead to complications such as cataract, glaucoma, macular swelling, or permanent vision loss. Early diagnosis and consistent treatment help control the inflammation and protect your vision.
What are the common symptoms of uveitis?
The most common symptoms are eye pain, redness, watering, and blurred vision. Some people also notice light sensitivity or floaters, especially in intermediate and posterior uveitis. Symptoms can appear suddenly or build up gradually, so any persistent eye redness with pain or reduced vision should be examined promptly.
What causes uveitis?
Uveitis can be linked to autoimmune conditions such as ankylosing spondylitis, rheumatoid arthritis, and psoriasis, as well as infections like tuberculosis, syphilis, herpes, and toxoplasmosis. Eye injury or previous eye surgery can also trigger it. In some patients, no specific cause is found even after detailed investigation.
Can uveitis come back after treatment?
Yes. Uveitis has a tendency to recur, which is why long-term treatment and regular follow-ups are important. Your doctor will gradually adjust medicines, monitor for signs of recurrence, and manage any complications early. Do not stop or change your medicines without medical advice, even if your eye feels better.
Which tests are used to diagnose uveitis?
Diagnosis begins with a detailed eye examination using a slit lamp, followed by tests such as OCT, FFA, B-scan ultrasonography, indirect ophthalmoscopy, and 90D biomicroscopy to assess the retina and deeper structures. Blood tests or chest X-rays may also be advised to look for underlying infections or autoimmune conditions.
