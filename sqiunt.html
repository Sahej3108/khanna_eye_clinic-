Children's Eye Health

Children may not always recognise or communicate that they are having difficulty seeing. Regular eye examinations and attention to changes in their eye appearance, behaviour, or visual habits can help identify problems early and allow timely treatment.

Signs Your Child May Need an Eye Check-Up

Certain changes in your child's eyes or everyday behaviour may indicate an underlying vision problem and should be evaluated by an eye specialist.

Misaligned or Wandering Eyes

One eye may turn inward, outward, upward, or downward, either constantly or occasionally. This may be a sign of squint or another eye movement problem.

Changes in Eye Appearance

A difference in pupil size, swelling, redness, crusting, or unusual changes around the eyes may require medical evaluation.

Unusual Pupil Reflection

A white reflection in the pupil instead of the usual red reflection in a child's photograph should be assessed promptly by an eye specialist.

Frequent Eye Rubbing or Blinking

Repeated eye rubbing or blinking may be associated with eye irritation, vision problems, or other ocular conditions.

Difficulty Seeing Clearly

Squinting, peering at distant objects, sitting unusually close to the television, or holding books very close to the face may indicate difficulty seeing clearly.

Difficulty in Dim Light

Frequent bumping into objects, stumbling, or having difficulty moving around in dimly lit areas can sometimes indicate a vision problem.

Understanding Lazy Eye and Squint

Early identification of visual development problems can help ensure that both eyes have the opportunity to develop and function together properly.

Lazy Eye (Amblyopia)

Lazy eye, or amblyopia, occurs when vision does not develop normally in one or both eyes during childhood. The brain may favour the clearer image from one eye and suppress the image from the weaker eye, resulting in reduced vision.

Squint (Strabismus)

Squint, medically known as strabismus, occurs when the two eyes are not properly aligned and point in different directions. One eye may look straight ahead while the other turns inward, outward, upward, or downward.

Eye Coordination Problems

When the eyes do not work together properly, a child may have difficulty maintaining focus or coordinating vision between both eyes. Early assessment can help determine the cause and appropriate management.

Childhood Vision Problems

Uncorrected vision problems during childhood can interfere with visual development and may contribute to amblyopia.

Near-Sightedness

Myopia makes distant objects appear blurred while objects closer to the child may be seen more clearly.

Far-Sightedness

Hypermetropia, or long-sightedness, can make it difficult to focus clearly, particularly on nearby objects. Significant hypermetropia may affect both near and distance vision.

Astigmatism

Astigmatism occurs when the cornea has an irregular curvature, causing blurred or distorted vision at different distances.

Amblyopia

When a significant vision problem remains uncorrected during the critical years of visual development, the weaker eye may fail to develop normal vision, resulting in amblyopia.

Non-Surgical Management for Better Vision

Many childhood eye conditions can be managed without surgery when detected and treated appropriately.

Regular Vision Assessment

Periodic eye examinations help assess visual development and determine the correct spectacle prescription as the child's eyes change.

Spectacle Correction

Glasses can correct common refractive errors such as myopia, hypermetropia, and astigmatism and help provide clearer vision.

Eye Exercises

Specific exercises may be recommended by the ophthalmologist for selected children depending on their individual eye condition.

Amblyopia Therapy

Treatment for amblyopia may include methods such as patching or other therapies recommended by the ophthalmologist to encourage the weaker eye to develop better vision.

Prism Correction

Prisms may be prescribed in selected cases to help manage certain eye alignment and coordination problems.

Convergence and Accommodation Management

Specific management techniques may be used to address problems involving the way the eyes focus and work together at different distances.

Surgical Management of Squint

When appropriate non-surgical measures do not adequately correct the misalignment, surgery may be considered to improve eye alignment and coordination.

Strabismus Surgery

Strabismus surgery involves adjusting the position or strength of the extraocular muscles to improve the alignment of the eyes.

Recession Procedure

In a recession procedure, an overactive eye muscle is detached and reattached farther back on the eye to reduce its pulling strength.

Resection Procedure

Resection strengthens a selected eye muscle by shortening it and repositioning it to improve the alignment of the eye.

Adjustable Sutures

In selected cases, adjustable sutures may allow the surgeon to make minor postoperative adjustments to achieve the desired eye position.
